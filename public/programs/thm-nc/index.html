Overview

The Th.M. in Nouthetic Counseling program is a focused study of the principles of Nouthetic Counseling designed for pastors who would like advanced training in this important pastoral task as well as graduates of secular counseling programs who would like to transition into a Nouthetic framework. The Th.M. in Nouthetic Counseling grants advanced standing in the Th.D. in Nouthetic Counseling program.

Admission

Requirement: M.A./M.S. in a counseling or social work field; or LCS MTS; or M.Div. Students with a thesis completed for their previous master’s program are exempt from the BS690 thesis requirement. Students with a secular counseling degree must complete TH701 – “Theological Foundations” as a prerequisite. Program is open to both male and female students. No transfer credit is allowed due to the uniqueness of the program.

Every applicant must also be a communing member in good standing of a local church, present a letter of recommendation from a church officer, and propose a qualified mentor. Full admission requirements.

Program requirements

  • All written assignments (with the exception of the thesis) should follow current MLA formatting.
  • All numbered assignments (lecture outlines/notes, summaries, chapter summaries, papers, etc.) must be submitted to your mentor for grading.

·
Assignment
Students with a thesis completed for their previous master’s program are exempt from the thesis requirement provided that they submit a copy of their previous thesis to the seminary.
·
Assignment
Students without a previous thesis must complete a thesis as outlined below: Your thesis topic must be directly related to the field of counseling. You must work closely with your mentor in developing your thesis topic and in working through the thesis. Thesis topics must first be approved by the seminary administration. Students must use the Request for Approval of Thesis Topic Form when submitting their topic for approval. The completed form should be emailed to info@logcollege.net. Students should not begin thesis work until the seminary administration has first approved the topic.
