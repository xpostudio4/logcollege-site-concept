Overview

The MTS program provides students with advanced theological study and will equip the student to critically engage in a wide range of theological fields. The MTS program consists of 61 academic credit hours and is comprised of select courses taken from the M.Div. program. The program requires a baccalaureate degree (in any field) or equivalent for admission.

Admission

Requirement: Bachelor degree (in any field) or equivalent; 40 hours maximum transfer credit allowed.

Every applicant must also be a communing member in good standing of a local church, present a letter of recommendation from a church officer, and propose a qualified mentor. Full admission requirements.

Program requirements

  • Complete the Comprehensive Final Exam
  • All written assignments (with the exception of the thesis) should follow current MLA formatting.
  • All numbered assignments (lecture outlines/notes, summaries, chapter summaries, papers, etc.) must be submitted to your mentor for grading.

BS690Master's Thesis6 credit hours
·
Assignment
You must work closely with your mentor in developing your thesis topic and in working through the thesis. Thesis topics must first be approved by the seminary administration. Students must use the Request for Approval of Thesis Topic Form when submitting their topic for approval. The completed form should be emailed to info@logcollege.net. Students should not begin thesis work until the seminary administration has first approved the topic.
·
Assignment
FORMATTING: Current Turabian
1
Assignment
Your Master's Thesis should be no less than 100 pages, 1 inch margins, twelve-point type, Times New Roman font, double spaced.
2
Assignment
Your Master's Thesis should be free of grammatical and syntax errors and should represent the caliber of work you have completed during your Master's Program.
3
Assignment
Your Master's Thesis must include a submission page, table of contents, and full bibliography.
4
Assignment
Your Master's Thesis must be submitted to LCS administration by your mentor (after grading) for final review and approval before you will be considered for graduation.
