Lectures

  • A Call to the Ministry 1-5 (opens in a new tab) — Albert Martin5 h
  • Call to Ministry 1-4 (opens in a new tab) — J.I. Packer et al.4 h 30 min

Reading
Read:
  • Address to Students of Divinity (first section of A Compendious View of Natural and Revealed Religion) (opens in a new tab) — John Brown of Haddington16 pp.
  • Scripture Doctrine of a Call to the Work of the Gospel Ministry (opens in a new tab) — W.S. Plumer50 pp.
  • The Qualifications of the Christian Ministry (pp. 35-46 of “The Christian Ministry”) (opens in a new tab) — Charles Bridges12 pp.
  • The Importance of a Thorough and Adequate Course of Preparatory Study for the Holy Ministry (opens in a new tab) — Samuel Miller58 pp.
  • An Address to Candidates for the Ministry (opens in a new tab) — Archibald Alexander46 pp.

  • The Danger of an Unconverted Ministry (opens in a new tab) — Gilbert Tennent20 pp.
Total: 216 pages
2
Assignment
Complete: James Nisbet's Spiritual Checklist. Answer all 31 questions. Based upon these questions and your answers to them, write a personal evaluation identifying your strengths as well as areas where growth is needed. The evaluation should be a minimum of one page in length and presented to your mentor for grading.
3
Assignment
Present to your mentor a written summary of your own personal call to ministry. The summary should include pertinent quotes from the lectures and reading material (cited according to MLA formatting). The summary should be a minimum of 1 page in length.
12 lecture hours · 216 pages · 1 written assignment

Required in

  • Master of Divinity
  • Bachelor of Divinity
All numbered assignments are submitted to your mentor for grading.

Study with a mentor, in your own church.

No tuition, no relocation, textbooks provided.
